# Bid Ladder — Product Thesis

## Core Question

> What's getting attention right now?

## Participant Question

> Can I get mine higher?

## Product Promise

- **The board is the product.** The homepage must show discovery immediately, not a marketing landing page.
- **Competition must be real.** No fabricated bids, users, clicks, rankings, activity, or social proof.
- **Status is valuable.** A listing should communicate meaningful status: rank, movement, visits, category position.
- **Movement creates retention.** Surface rising listings, new listings, overtakes, rank changes, click activity, category movement.
- **Discovery before monetization.** Visitors understand the product before being asked to spend.
- **Simplicity.** Features are added only when they strengthen the core loop.
- **Experiment instead of assuming.** Unknowns become measurable experiments.

## Emotional Model

The product should create legitimate emotional triggers through actual marketplace behavior:

- **Curiosity** — “What is getting attention?”
- **Status** — “We're #7.”
- **Competition** — “We can pass #6.”
- **FOMO** — “Someone just passed us.”
- **Pride** — “We're one of the top companies in our category.”
- **Belonging** — “Our company is part of this ecosystem.”
- **Recognition** — “We were spotted.”

## Anti-Features

- Task/reward marketplace mechanics.
- Fake popularity or ranking manipulation.
- Cryptocurrency or token mechanics.
- Enterprise analytics dashboards as primary UX.
- Social network functionality.
